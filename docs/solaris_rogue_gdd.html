1. Vision

You command a mercenary unit that is stranded and in debt. Its way out is a stable on Solaris VII, the Game World. A run is one Solaris season. Fight through the Light, Medium, Heavy and Assault circuits, then the seven rounds of the Grand Tournament. Keep up the lender's payments, and don't lose your last MechWarrior on the way.

The run structure comes from PokéRogue: a fixed ladder of bouts split into circuits, a champion at the end of each circuit, branching choices of where to go next, a pick of three rewards after every win, catching new units in the middle of a run, and meta-unlocks that carry across runs. Every bout is played under real Classic BattleTech rules by the btlib10x engine, through a graphical hex interface or a fast auto-resolve.

Every bout matters

Damage, ammo and pilot injuries carry over. A win that costs you an arm pays less than it looks.

The stable is your company

You start with a lance of 'Mech bays and can grow to a full company of twelve. You build the roster by salvaging, recruiting, buying and event rewards.

Real BattleTech rules

Total Warfare rules from btlib10x: heat, crits, piloting skill rolls, cluster hits. There is no simplified combat model.

Platform. A standalone single-player web game. It runs in the browser, works offline once loaded, and has no server or account. All saves are local.

Pacing: why 27 bouts, not 50 or 200

PokéRogue's wave count only works because a wave is very short. We found no published figure for turns or seconds per wave. The code has no wave timer, and its only speed controls are the game-speed setting (2–5) and options to skip animations and EXP bars. Speedrun listings show Classic runs of roughly 22–50 minutes for 200 waves, but only from search titles, so the figures are unverified. Either way, a PokéRogue wave lasts seconds. A BattleTech duel does not.

At about 8 minutes a duel, 50 bouts would take 6–7 hours, which is longer than most players will sit through for a run they can lose. 27 bouts take about 3.5 hours fully tactical, or 1.5–2 hours if routine league bouts are auto-resolved. That is in line with MW4's 17-match ladder and short enough to replay. It is also built only from Solaris units: four class circuits of five, and the canon seven-round Grand Tournament.

3b. Solaris VII: the setting

Solaris VII is a Terra-sized world on the Lyran–Free Worlds border. It is nominally Lyran and run by a Civic Council. Solaris City is divided into sectors, each backed by one of the Great Houses and each with one Class Six (Open) arena. (sarna.net)

League structure (canon anchors we use)

  • Classes: Two is Light, Three Medium, Four Heavy, Five Assault, and Six is Open (any weight, fought only in the five House arenas).
  • Licences and fees: a dueling licence costs 5,000–50,000 C-bills. Entry fees are 500–2,500 C-bills. League bouts score 3 / 1 / 0. Each class publishes a Top 20 every year.
  • Grand Tournament: 128 warriors, single elimination, Open class. The winner takes 500,000 C-bills and becomes the Class Six Champion. Seven rounds means seven bouts, which maps directly onto the last seven bouts of the season (§6.1).
  • Stables hold 10–40 warriors. The owner provides the 'Mechs and support and takes 15–25% of direct revenue plus merchandising and broadcast rights.
  • Betting: odds combine the 'Mech's strength and the pilot's skill. There are official terminals and underground bookies. Fixed fights and skimmed purses are canon plot hooks.
  • Surrender: a pilot yields by radio and flare. A surrender bond of 50,000–250,000 C-bills is forfeited on yielding. Fights are "not to the death," though deaths happen. An illegal underground circuit exists alongside the sanctioned league.
  • Other formats: grand mêlées of 3–6 warriors, team and tag-team lances, Weapons Dead (melee only), and novelty events.
Lore hygiene. Only use stable names that are on sarna's list of Solaris stables (for example Cenotaph, Blackstar, Skye Tigers, Starlight, Black Ronin, Zellbrigen, Gilgamesh, Ice Giants, Red Jians, Wyren Claws) when claiming something is canon. Rival stables made up for the game must be marked as original. Canon champions depend on the era (for example Gray Noton 3016–22, Justin Xiang 3027). Check every named NPC against the era pack's year.

3d. Engine defects the game should expect (260930 report)

The analysis of 260930-345927f (report/bt10x_engine_analysis_260930-345927f.html) found 16 findings: 5 high, 10 medium and 1 low. All were reproduced against the live engine. The design rule is that the game never re-implements or patches a rule. The UI and AI ask the engine what is legal (validateAttacks, validatePhysicalAttack, reachableLandings), so play always matches the engine, and every library fix reaches the game without changes on our side. An engine upgrade is a deliberate step: re-run the game's battle regression scenarios (built the same way as ref/scenarios/<version>/) and re-check the AI weights. 4. Core loop

A loss does not end the run. You lose the purse and fame, may lose your bet or surrender bond, and keep whatever damage you took. The run ends only when the stable cannot field any legal pilot and 'Mech pair for the next bout and cannot afford repairs, or when it misses a loan instalment after its grace (§10.4). This matches PokéRogue, where the run ends when the whole party faints, not when one wave goes badly.

6. Run structure & modes

6.1 Classic: one Solaris season, 27 bouts (D14)

  • Circuits. Each circuit is 4 league bouts, scored 3 / 1 / 0 for win, judged draw and loss as in canon, followed by bout 5. With at least 6 league points (two wins) bout 5 is a title shot against the reigning class champion. With fewer, it is a contender's bout against a ranked fighter, with a smaller purse and no title.
  • Class gates. In a Class N bout, both 'Mechs must fall within that class's tonnage band (§17). Class 6 has no limit. You need a fighting 'Mech for each circuit's class by the time it starts, and you begin with only four bays (§7.1), so planning the roster is the season's main strategic problem.
  • Licences. Each circuit needs its class licence, bought before bout 1 of the circuit. Fees scale with class, from 5,000 to 50,000 C-bills, as in canon.
  • Choosing the host sector. Before each circuit you choose which sector hosts it, from 2–3 options. This is where PokéRogue's biome links come in. The sector decides the arena pool, which House sponsor is favored, the event deck and the terrain palette. The Grand Tournament rotates through the five House Open arenas.
  • Bout card. Each league bout offers 2 match-ups to choose from, differing in arena, opponent doctrine, purse and odds. Title, contender, rival and Grand Tournament bouts are fixed.
  • Rival stable. A recurring rival that the game invents (not canon) at bout 3 of each circuit, plus a possible Grand Tournament meeting. It keeps its own roster and its own damage between meetings.
  • Champions. The reigning class champions have named profiles (§9.3), a signature 'Mech, elite skills and a bigger purse.
  • Grand Tournament. Bouts 21–27 are the seven knockout rounds (128 → 64 → 32 → 16 → 8 → 4 → 2 → 1). Seeding improves with each class title you won, so a champion stable meets weaker early opponents. Losing a Grand Tournament bout ends the season as "eliminated." It still counts as a completed run for meta rewards, scaled by the round you reached.
  • Chassis gap. The Light and Heavy circuits can't be built until the library gets more 3025 chassis (R1, §3c). Until then the vertical slice is the Medium circuit (§18).

7. The stable

7.1 Roster: lance to company (D12)

  • 'Mech bays. You start by leasing 4 bays in a Solaris hangar, one lance. More bays are leased from the market or won as rewards, in steps, up to 12 bays, a full company of three lances. Each bay has a lease cost per bout (§17). A 'Mech that has no bay must be sold or left in unpaid storage, where it cannot be repaired or fielded.
  • MechWarriors. You can keep up to bays + 2 pilots: one per 'Mech plus two dispossessed reserves, who are a normal sight on Solaris. That is 6 at the start and 14 for a full company. Canon stables hold 10–40 warriors, so a full company makes a modest stable.
  • Fielding. Duels use one lance member. Tag-team bouts use two. Off-world contracts field a whole lance of four, which is why four is the starting bay count.
  • Pilots and 'Mechs are separate. A pilot is assigned to a 'Mech before each bout. Swapping is free, but a pilot fighting outside their preferred class (§7.2) takes a morale penalty.
  • Clan-era pack. An optional Clan-exile stable uses Clan formations: Point (1) → Star (5) → Binary (10) → Trinary (15). They do not appear before the 3050s pack, because the Inner Sphere learned of the Clans only in 3048–50.

7.3 'Mechs and persistent damage

  • A stable 'Mech record holds model, damage (armor, rear armor, internal structure per location), critSlotsDestroyed, destroyedWeapons, ammoBins, and its history (kills, bouts fought). These are the same fields the engine uses at :9468–9504, so results copy back field for field.
  • After a bout, the game copies the unit's end state into the stable record. Heat, prone status and shutdown do not carry over. They are cleared as the 'Mech powers down and is recovered.
  • A destroyed 'Mech is not deleted. It becomes a wreck that can be repaired, but the bill is huge and grows with every location lost. A 'Mech whose center torso is gone, or whose engine is destroyed, is scrap only: it gives parts credit and one Technician TRO page for the codex (§14.1).
  • Pilot death (D6): the cockpitDestroyed event, or pilot hits reaching 6 (for example through headBlownOff or ammo-explosion feedback). Ejecting (ejectPilot :11283) saves the pilot, gives up the bout and costs fame.

8.2 Auto-resolve

Auto-resolve does not use a simplified formula. It runs the real engine with the AI controlling both sides. The player's side uses each MechWarrior's assigned doctrine (§9.3), which the player sets per pilot. There are no animations, so a whole bout resolves in well under a second. The results are exactly as trustworthy as tactical play, and the event log can be replayed afterwards.

  • The player can take control at the start of any turn, or set rules like "take over when armor drops below 50%."
  • Auto-resolve is always allowed for normal bouts. For champion and Grand Tournament bouts it is allowed but shows a warning, because the AI doesn't play as well as a human.

8.3 Victory conditions (game layer, via stopper)

  1. Knockout: every enemy unit is destroyed, removed or ejected (isUnitDestroyed, isUnitRemoved).
  2. Disabled: a unit is immobile (isUnitImmobile) and has no working weapon with line of sight, or is shut down or unconscious for 2 turns in a row. The judges stop the bout.
  3. Yield: a player button. The AI yields when its doctrine's threshold is crossed (§9.2).
  4. Ring-out: a unit standing outside the map after a push loses as if it had yielded (§3d, PHY-08).
  5. Time limit: 12 turns in a duel (the tuning default). After that, judges decide by the share of armor and structure lost plus damage dealt. A judged draw scores 1 point in the league.

8.6 Speed and presentation

9. Opponent AI: MechWarrior doctrines

The AI is a one-ply scoring AI in the game layer, built on the engine's helpers. On each move it gets candidate end hexes from reachableLandings, scores each one with the doctrine's weights, and commits the best. On each attack it starts from suggestFireTargets / bestFireTarget, then applies the doctrine's heat ceiling and ammo discipline to the weapon list. When the target is adjacent, it also scores the legal physical options (validatePhysicalAttack, with expected damage from computePhysicalToHit) against the weapons those limbs would have to give up, weighted by the doctrine's melee preference. Each unit gets one target per turn (§3c). The CLI's autoplay.ts heuristics (approach, face the target, fire the best set) are the baseline to beat.

A doctrine is a MechWarrior's habit, not a 'Mech's role. The same doctrine table drives opponents, champions and your own MechWarriors in auto-resolve (§8.2), so choosing a pilot's doctrine is also an economic choice: a Dispossessed veteran brings the 'Mech home with ammo left in the bins.

9.1 Scoring terms

      - w.heat  * projectedHeatOver(heatCeiling)
// an ammo weapon is only considered if its target number ≤ the doctrine's ammo threshold
// and its bin stays above the doctrine's reserve, except in the final turns of a time-limited bout

10.2 Fame

  • Stable fame (0–1000) is the run's reputation, standing in for the canon Top 20 rankings. It unlocks sponsor tiers, higher bookie stake limits, better recruits and merchandising income.
  • Fame gained in a bout is read from the engine's event stream: win × underdogFactor + crowdPoints. crowdPoints reward headBlownOff, ammoExplosion on the opponent, legBlownOff followed by unitFell, and finishing the fight under the time limit. Yielding costs fame, and a quick yield costs more.

10.3 Odds and betting

payout     = stake × (1 / p) × 0.9                               // 10% house edge

  • You can only bet on your own pilot's bouts. The bookie's view does not see pilot traits, doctrine or the arena, and that is where a skilled player finds an edge.
  • Underdog factor = clamp(1/p − 1, 0, 2) multiplies both the purse bonus and the fame for a win.
  • BV comes from the game layer's own table (D7). 10.4 Debt (mercenary backstory, D8)

The unit arrives on Solaris with nothing but its MechWarriors and a debt for DropShip passage and the hangar deposit. A Cathay lender offers a credit line to buy the opening stable (D15, §14.2). Every C-bill spent is added to the loan. A bigger starting stable means heavier instalments, so the starter screen is already the first economic decision of the run.

  • Instalments. One is due after each circuit (bouts 5, 10, 15 and 20): a fixed share of the starting principal plus interest on the balance (§17). If you miss one, you get one grace, which triggers the Loan Shark event (§13). Missing a second ends the run as repossessed.
  • Season's end. The lender rolls the remaining balance over if your stable's net worth (the resale value of your 'Mechs at their current condition, plus cash) covers it. That rolled-over balance is what Endless carries into the next season. The Classic score is your Grand Tournament finish plus net worth minus debt.
  • Paying it off early earns a large fame bonus and the "Debt Free" achievement.

The debt gives the run economic pressure, the way PokéRogue's rising enemy levels give it combat pressure. It is also the classic mercenary situation: the 'Mechs are worth millions, the purses are worth thousands, and the gap is covered by salvage, bets and nerve.

12. Acquisition (D5: all four)

12.1 Salvage

  • After a win you may claim the loser's 'Mech as it was when the bout ended: its damage, crits and remaining ammo. The claim costs a fee of 25% of the 'Mech's canon list price, adjusted by a Scrapper trait or salvage-rights items. Champion 'Mechs cannot be claimed; you get that chassis's Classified TRO instead (§14.1).
  • A claimed 'Mech can be kept (it needs a bay) or sold on the market for 40% of list price, scaled by its condition. Selling salvage is a real source of income, as it is for any mercenary. The claim fee keeps it from being free money.
  • How brutal the win was decides what you get: a knockout gives a wreck that needs expensive repairs, while forcing a yield gives a 'Mech that is nearly intact. This rewards clean wins, much like PokéRogue making you weaken a Pokémon without making it faint.
  • Salvaging a chassis for the first time adds it to the codex (§14.1).

12.3 Market and auction

The market is between bouts, in the hub. Its stock changes every 2 bouts and grows by circuit, like PokéRogue's shop brackets. 'Mechs sell at canon list price adjusted by condition. Bay leases are sold here too (§7.1).

  • Circuit I: ammo, armor, med-kits, used ENF-4R and HBK-4G, light chassis (needs R1)
  • Circuit II and later: jump-capable medium 'Mechs, MechWarriors for hire
  • Circuit III and later: heavy chassis (needs R1)
  • Circuit IV and later: assault chassis

An auction event now and then offers a rare 'Mech against AI bidders.

12.4 Events and contracts

Events can reward 'Mechs or pilots (§13). Off-world contracts can bring back battlefield salvage from outside the arena.

12.5 Heirloom 'Mechs (D18)

In the Succession Wars, a BattleMech is often a family's whole fortune, passed down for generations. An heirloom is a 'Mech that comes with its own history: a recorded line of former pilots, kill marks on the torso, a name the crowd knows. It plays the part of PokéRogue's shiny Pokémon. The rules are the same as any other 'Mech of its model, but it is worth more in fame and money.

13. Events (the Mystery Encounter equivalent)

Events can fire in the hub between bouts 2 and 26. Each one is a narrative choice with 2–4 options, some needing a particular pilot trait, 'Mech, cash or fame level. The spawn chance works like PokéRogue's pity ramp:

pEvent(bout) = 15% + 10% × boutsSinceLastEvent   // ≈ 6–7 events per 27-bout season

Event tiers are Common, Uncommon and Rare, weighted 66 / 19 / 3 as in PokéRogue. A Rare event can happen at most once per run.

14.3 Hall of Fame and legacy

A pilot who survives to the end of a run (the Classic season finished, eliminated in the Grand Tournament, or a full season in Endless), or who retires with 12 or more wins, joins the Hall of Fame. Hall of Fame pilots can be taken as starters with their skills reduced by one step. Each passes on one trait to a trainee. A pilot who dies gets a memorial entry and cannot be used again.

14.4 Memory-core leads and Equatus expeditions (D17)

This is the counterpart of PokéRogue's eggs and vouchers, with the gacha vocabulary removed. The canon model is the Helm Memory Core, a Star League data store found in 3028 that became the main route for recovering lostech. Solaris VII's dry, mountainous second continent, Equatus, is where the rumours point.

  • Leads are earned from a first title win in each class, a first finished season, the first Daily run each day, and achievements. They are also a Lostech settlement lot (§11) and come from the Lostech Rumor event (§13). A lead funds one or more expeditions:
    • Rumor: 1 expedition
    • Survey: 5
    • Charter: 10, at least one Depot or better guaranteed
    • Grand Expedition: 25, at least one Castle Brian or better guaranteed
  • An expedition returns after N bouts played, in any mode. What it finds is rolled out of 256 when it sets out, using PokéRogue's egg odds and pity counters:

15.2 RNG

Each run has a single seeded PRNG behind the engine's RNG interface { d6(), snapshot(), load(s) }, the same shape as the CLI's generator. Game-layer rolls (rewards, events, recruiting) come from a separate stream seeded from the run seed, so reloading a battle cannot change the reward rolls. The engine's DEFAULT_RNG must never be used (§3c).

15.5 Saves

  • System data: codex and TRO clearances, Hall of Fame and heirloom histories, memory-core leads and expeditions under way, achievements, settings, run history.
  • Session data: the whole run state. It is auto-saved at every hub visit and at the start of every turn during a battle (saveBattleState JSON plus both RNG snapshots). There is one save slot per mode. Loading resumes exactly where you left off, and because the RNG is saved too, reloading a turn does not change the dice (save-scumming does nothing).
  • Saves can be exported and imported as a JSON file, since there are no accounts.
